Supplement vs. medicine

Supplements

Meant to add to the diet. They are not approved to treat disease, and they are regulated differently from drugs.

Medicines

Tested and approved for specific conditions, and prescribed or advised by a clinician.

What this means for you

Use a supplement as support only, and never stop a prescribed medicine without your doctor's advice.

Daily Wellness for a Healthier Brain

The habits below have solid support in general health research. They work with or without any supplement.

Sleep 7–9 hours

Sleep is when the brain sorts and stores what you learned. Keep a steady bedtime and keep screens out of the last hour.

Move every day

Brisk walking, cycling or swimming improves blood flow. Aim for about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week.

Eat for your brain

Favor vegetables, berries, nuts, beans, whole grains and fish. Limit heavily processed food and excess sugar.

Drink water

Even mild dehydration can affect concentration. Keep a bottle within reach.

Keep learning

Pick up a language, an instrument, puzzles or a new skill. Novelty gives the brain a workout.

Stay connected

Regular time with friends and family supports mood and mental engagement.

Manage stress

Try ten minutes of breathing, meditation or a quiet walk. Chronic stress makes focus harder.

Get checkups

Blood pressure, blood sugar, hearing and vision all affect brain health. Review them with your doctor.

A simple sample day

  1. Morning: Drink a glass of water, eat a balanced breakfast and take your supplement as directed on the label.
  2. Midday: Take a 20-minute walk outside and eat a lunch with vegetables and protein.
  3. Afternoon: Work in 45-minute blocks with short breaks to stay focused.
  4. Evening: Call someone, read or do a puzzle, then wind down without screens.

Simple memory tricks

  1. Say it out loud. Repeat names when you meet someone.
  2. Chunk it. Break long numbers into small groups.
  3. Link it. Connect new facts to something you already know.
  4. Write it down. Lists and calendars free up mental space.
  5. Review it. Revisit new material after a day, then a week.

Small routines build up. Add MemoClear only if it suits you and your doctor agrees.
